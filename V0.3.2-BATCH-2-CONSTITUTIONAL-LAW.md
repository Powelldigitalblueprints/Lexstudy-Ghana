### Discrepancies found between sources (items 1–2 resolved in review; the rest are presentation notes)

1. Electoral Commission appointments — RESOLVED in review (Muse,
   2026-11-13). Wiafe §14 lists the Electoral Commission
   chairman/deputies/members under article 70 "in consultation with the
   Council of State". Adjei Ch. 15 (PDF p. 408) treats them as article
   70(2), "on the advice of" the Council of State — the only article 70
   appointment made on advice. The Constitution text confirms the Adjei
   reading: article 70(1) lists the consultation appointments
   (Commissioner for Human Rights and Administrative Justice,
   Auditor-General, District Assemblies Common Fund Administrator,
   commissions and other offices) and article 70(2) provides that the
   President, "acting on the advice of the Council of State", appoints
   the Chairman, Deputy Chairmen and other members of the Electoral
   Commission (1992 Constitution, Chapter 8 text; the Electoral
   Commission's own website states the same). The draft follows the
   Constitution text. No further check needed.
2. C.I. 47 year — RESOLVED in review (Muse, 2026-11-13). Slides 6 and 8
   say "High Court Civil Procedure Rules, 2014 (C.I. 47)"; slide 11 and
   Adjei Ch. 10 (PDF p. 304) say 2004. The Rules are the High Court
   (Civil Procedure) Rules, 2004 (C.I. 47), made 1 June 2004 (WIPO Lex;
   Parliament of Ghana repository); Order 55 is judicial review and
   Order 56 is habeas corpus. The slides' "2014" is an error. The draft
   uses 2004. No further check needed.
3. Order 55 vs Order 56: the slides and Adjei agree — Order 55 (C.I. 47)
   sets out the judicial-review remedies; Order 56 (C.I. 47) governs
   habeas corpus. Habeas corpus forms part of the courts' supervisory
   powers but is not itself a judicial-review remedy (Adjei, PDF p. 304).
4. "What is a constitution?" characterisations: the sources support
   written, rigid and supreme, and "written, unitary and republican"
   (Adjei, PDF p. 26; article 4: Ghana is a unitary Republic). The draft
   states "presidential" from article 57(1) (the President is Head of
   State, Head of Government and Commander-in-Chief) and "single-chamber
   Parliament" from article 93 (one Parliament is established) — both
   phrased from Constitution text, not asserted as source quotations.
5. The source notes describe the Ghana Lotto position on DPSP
   justiciability as "authoritative". The draft does NOT adopt that: all
   three recorded positions are presented side by side, honestly, with
   their flags.
6. The proposition that the President "cannot appoint a non-recommended
   person" (Judicial Council recommendations) is the Wiafe notes author's
   own argument ("my understanding"), not a verified holding. The draft
   note presents it labelled as the notes' argument.
7. Historical points taken from the notes are flagged "as presented in
   the source notes": 1992 Constitution in force 7 January 1993;
   referendum figures for entrenched amendments (40% turnout, 75% in
   favour); Transitional Provisions sections 34, 35 and 37 unamendable;
   Supreme Court Ordinance No. 4 of 1876, section 14 reception clause
   (English law in force 24 July 1874).
